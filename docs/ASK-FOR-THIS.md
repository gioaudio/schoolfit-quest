# What to ask for — one school at a time

Copy everything below the line into a fresh chat. Change the school name. That's it.

Six questions. Roughly one page of answers per school. 22 schools.

---

**School: [NAME], Perth, Western Australia**

Answer these six questions about this school. Perth, WA — not any interstate or overseas school with a similar name.

**1 · The numbers** *(My School — myschool.edu.au)*
- Enrolment, Years 7–12
- ICSEA value
- Teaching staff FTE

**2 · The cost**
- Year 7 tuition, and the year it applies to
- Total compulsory extras on top — levies, resource fees, IT charges. One figure is fine.

**3 · What they offer**

For each of **sport**, **music**, **visual art and drama**, **computing and robotics**:
- What exists? Name the actual subjects, teams, ensembles, clubs.
- What's a standout — a specialist program, a competition record, a facility, real scale?
- **Who can get in?** Open to anyone, or audition, trial, portfolio or invitation? *This is the most important part of the question.* A school with a superb orchestra that only takes auditioned players is a completely different place, for my child, from one where anyone can join.
- Any numbers on participation — "400 students learn an instrument", "18 netball teams".

**3b · Music — what kind, specifically**

Don't answer "they have a strong music program". Two schools can both be strong and suit completely different children. Go through these seven and say whether each exists, and whether it's a standout:

- **Orchestral** — strings, symphony or chamber orchestra
- **Concert / wind band**
- **Choral** — choirs, vocal ensembles
- **Jazz** — swing band, jazz combo
- **Contemporary** — rock or pop bands, amps, contemporary ensembles, songwriting
- **Music production** — recording, studio, DAWs, sound engineering, music technology as a subject
- **Instrumental tuition** — one-to-one lessons, which instruments, cost per lesson

**Contemporary and production are the two I most need and the two most often missing.** If a school is entirely classical and liturgical, say so plainly — that's a real and useful finding, not a gap. Do not assume a big music department covers everything.

**4 · Academic — how they teach, and what they actually offer**

- Is there a **selective or gifted program**? How do you get in — test, application, catchment?
- Is there **streaming or setting**? Which subjects, from which year, and how are students placed?
- What happens for a student who's **ahead**? And one who's **behind**?
- Is there a published teaching approach, instructional framework or coaching model?
- **Which of these are offered through to Year 12?** Maths (including Methods and Specialist), English and Literature, the sciences (which ones), HASS subjects, **languages (name them)**, visual art, drama, music, design and technology, **computer science or IT**, PE Studies.
- Roughly what's the **ATAR / VET balance** — is this a school where most students do ATAR, or a genuine mix?

The senior subject list matters because the quiz asks children to rate eleven subjects individually. A child who loves languages and a child who loves computing currently get treated identically, and they shouldn't.

**5 · Pastoral care — facts, not adjectives**
- What's the pastoral unit called, how many students in it, how often does it meet?
- **Does the same adult stay with a student across multiple years?** Answer `yes`, `no`, or `not stated`. Answer `no` if contact hands over at a year boundary. Warm language about care is not a yes.
- What's the published chain when a student struggles? e.g. "Tutor → Head of House → psychologist"

**6 · Beyond the school's own sales pitch**

This is the part I care most about, because everything above comes from the school itself.

- **If it's a public school:** find the **WA Public School Review** report. Quote what the external reviewers actually said about teaching quality, student support, and areas for improvement. Give the year and the URL. This is written by assessors with nothing to sell and it's the single most valuable source available.
- **If it's a private school:** no external review exists in WA. Check **SEEK, Glassdoor and Indeed** for reviews by *teaching staff*, 2023 or later. Quote them, with dates. Include the negative ones — if reviews contradict each other, quote both sides rather than averaging them out.

---

**Rules**

- **Quote it and link it.** For anything that isn't on the school's front page, give me the sentence and the URL.
- **"Not found" is a real answer and I want it.** Nine schools in this project turned out to have no independent staff reviews at all, and knowing that is genuinely useful. Don't pad.
- **Never estimate a number.** Don't infer a class size, a program, or a structure from a school's tone or from what similar schools do. This feeds a tool that Perth families will use to compare real schools — a plausible guess and a fact look identical to me afterwards, and I have no way to tell them apart.
- **Don't rate the school or tell me if it's good.** Facts only.

## Notes for me, not the chat

**Order to work through them.** The four thinnest records first, because empty data currently reads as "doesn't offer it": **Melville, Willetton, Applecross, Shenton** — all public, all with 0–1 music sub-domains against a private-school average of 5.

Then the ten with no independent evidence: Christ Church, Santa Maria, Wesley, Iona, Seton, Scotch, CBC Fremantle, Kennedy, Carey Harrisdale, Aquinas.

Then the rest: Perth Modern, John Curtin, Rossmoyne, Fremantle College, Hale, All Saints, Corpus Christi. *(John XXIII is done.)*

**Deliberately dropped, and why:**

| Dropped | Reason |
|---|---|
| Newsletter counting | Structurally biased — portal-locked schools score zero regardless of what they do. Measures publicity, not experience. |
| Reddit / Whirlpool / Facebook themes | Ten schools searched, almost nothing ever met the three-account threshold. Worst effort-to-yield in the project. |
| ACNC filings, headcount, vacancies | Inconsistently published, and two concurrent vacancies prove nothing about turnover. My School gives better staffing data. |
| Fee line items | Parked. One combined extras figure is enough until fees get revisited. |
| 17 individual sport sub-domains | Replaced by "what exists, what's a standout, who can get in" — same information, far less work. |

**The one field that matters most** is *who can get in*. It's nearly absent across all 21 existing records, and it's the difference between a program a child can join and one they can only watch.

**Why 3b and the subject list were added.** Everything here maps onto a question a child already answers — nothing is collected speculatively.

Q4 asks the child to choose *one* of seven things the music department should buy. Current coverage across 22 schools: one-to-one lessons 19, concert band 17, choir 14, orchestra 12, jazz 7, **rock band 7, recording and production 4**. The three contemporary options are the worst covered in the dataset, and a producer-first musician was the original brief for the whole tool.

Q9 asks the child to rate eleven subjects individually — maths, english, science, HASS, languages, art, music, drama, D&T, computing, PE. School-side there is a single `academic` number. That grid's signal is currently thrown away, and a senior subject list is the cheapest way to use it: it's published, factual, and one page per school.
